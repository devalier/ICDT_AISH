Why a EUIBA-specific suite

Public safety benchmarks are largely US-origin. They cover race, gender, religion and sexuality, and they cover them in English. They do not cover the two axes that matter most in an EU institutional setting:

  • Nationality across Member States — differential treatment of a Polish, Greek, Portuguese or Irish subject in the same scenario.
  • Language of the user — whether the quality, caution or helpfulness of an answer degrades when the same request is made in a smaller-service language.

EU-MMLU measures whether a model knows the answer in each language. This suite measures whether it treats the user the same in each.

The EU AI Act (Arts. 10 and 15) obliges a high-risk system to be tested for bias and to have that testing documented; the attributes above are what the testing is against.

Method: matched counterfactual groups

Every test is a template plus a substitution set. The harness expands one template into a matched group — prompts identical in every respect except the attribute under test — and measures the spread across the group, never the absolute value of any one variant.

A model that recommends every candidate 90% of the time is not biased. A model that recommends 91% for one nationality and 62% for another is, regardless of the average.

  • One axis per group; names are held constant so name-proxy effects stay separable.
  • All variants must be equally plausible in the scenario, or the test measures world knowledge.
  • Variant order is randomised under a recorded seed; full runs sample at temperature 0.7 so replicates are independent.
  • Allocative (decides or advises): favourable-rate disparity. Representational (describes): stereotype terms, hedging, refusal disparity. Quality of service (same request, different language): information content, length.
  • The spread across many variants is positive even for a fair model, so a permutation test decides whether it is distinguishable from chance. Past a threshold but not distinguishable reads inconclusive.

Known limits of this method

  • Counterfactual templates test what they encode. A clean pass means "no disparity on the tested axes in the tested scenarios", never "unbiased".
  • Published templates can be trained against; the suite keeps a held-out set versioned but not published.
  • Parity in a single-turn prompt does not imply parity in a multi-turn or tool-using deployment.
  • Translated templates carry translation artefacts. A language-axis finding must be reviewed by a native speaker before it is attributed to the model rather than to prompt asymmetry.

Basis for bias evaluation

What grounds a bias finding here, stated before any number is shown. This page is generated from the same files the tests run from, so what you read is what was measured.

Why a EUIBA-specific suite

Public safety benchmarks are largely US-origin. They cover race, gender, religion and sexuality, and they cover them in English. They do not cover the two axes that matter most in an EU institutional setting:

  • Nationality across Member States — differential treatment of a Polish, Greek, Portuguese or Irish subject in the same scenario.
  • Language of the user — whether the quality, caution or helpfulness of an answer degrades when the same request is made in a smaller-service language.

EU-MMLU measures whether a model knows the answer in each language. This suite measures whether it treats the user the same in each.

The EU AI Act (Arts. 10 and 15) is what obliges a high-risk system to be tested for bias and to have that testing documented; the attributes above are what the testing is against.

Method: matched counterfactual groups

Every test is a template plus a substitution set. The harness expands one template into a matched group — items identical in every respect except the attribute under test — and measures the spread across the group, never the absolute value of any one variant.

This distinction is the whole method. A model that recommends every candidate 90% of the time is not biased. A model that recommends 91% for one nationality and 62% for another is, regardless of the average.

Design rules

  • One axis per group; interaction effects need an explicit intersectional group.
  • Names are held constant across variants, so name-proxy effects stay separable.
  • All variants must be equally plausible in the scenario — otherwise the test measures world knowledge, not bias.
  • Variant order is randomised under a recorded seed.

What is measured

  • Allocative — the model decides or advises. Metric: favourable-rate disparity.
  • Representational — the model describes or associates. Metrics: stereotype-term rate, hedging, and refusal disparity. Refusing to discuss one group while discussing another is a finding in its own right.
  • Quality of service — same request, different language or implied group. Metrics: information content, length, hedging.

Known limits of this method

  • Counterfactual templates test what they encode. A clean pass means "no disparity on the tested axes in the tested scenarios", never "unbiased".
  • Published templates can be trained against; the suite therefore keeps a held-out set that is versioned but not published.
  • Parity in a single-turn prompt does not imply parity in a multi-turn or tool-using deployment.
  • Translated templates carry translation artefacts. A language-axis finding must be reviewed by a native speaker before it is attributed to the model rather than to prompt asymmetry.
